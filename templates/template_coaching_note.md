```markdown
---
type: coaching_note
register: direct, developmental, low-ceremony
audience: direct report (IC or manager)
when_to_use: >
  You observed a specific behavior — in a meeting, a deliverable, a communication, or
  a decision — that missed the mark or shows a growth edge. The note is not punitive.
  It names what happened, why it matters operationally, and exactly what you want
  different next time. Send within 48 hours of the triggering event while context is live.
---

## Structural Pattern

1. **Anchor** — name the specific event or artifact without preamble.
   No "I wanted to circle back on..." Just: the thing, the moment, the deliverable.

2. **Observation** — describe the behavior or gap as a fact, not a diagnosis of character.
   What was present or absent. What the reader or room received.

3. **Operational consequence** — state what breaks, degrades, or misleads when this continues.
   Not hypothetical. Grounded in the actual context.

4. **Rule or standard** — give the decision rule or principle that should govern this situation.
   "If X, then Y." This is the reusable takeaway, not a one-time correction.

5. **The ask** — one concrete, time-bounded action that demonstrates the shift.
   Not a general improvement goal. A specific thing to do or produce.

Optional: Brief show-of-confidence or framing if tone needs softening — but keep it one sentence, earned, not reflexive.

---

## Template

**[MEDIUM: email subject line if email / no subject if Teams chat]**

Subject (if email): [Topic that was observed — no editorializing in subject line]

---

[FIRST NAME] —

[1–2 sentences: name the specific event, deliverable, or exchange. Be precise — date, meeting name, document title, or message thread if relevant.]

[1–2 sentences: what you observed. Behavior, not intent. What was present (e.g., detail overload, deference to others, missing BLUF) or absent (e.g., no recommendation, no numbers, no ownership of a position).]

[1–2 sentences: the operational consequence. Who was confused, what decision slowed, what signal the audience received. Make it concrete — not "this could cause problems" but "this reads as X to an executive" or "without this, the date is undefendable."]

[The rule, written as a decision rule or standard: "If [situation], then [expected behavior]." This is what you want internalized, not just applied once.]

[The ask: one specific action. "Before [date/meeting/next occurrence], [do this specific thing]." If it's a document, describe what it should contain. If it's a behavior in meetings, describe the moment and what you want instead.]

[Optional: one-sentence framing that acknowledges why this person is getting the note — because you expect more, because this is a growth opportunity you're investing in, not because they're in trouble. Skip if the note already carries that tone.]

## Annotated Examples

### Example A — Executive communication standard (Teams chat, lightly annotated)

> **"Well, then use me as practice for executive communication: Teaching moment."**

*Anchor* — implicit: they just sent Joe a wall of detail intended for an exec audience.

> **"You cannot send out all of these details to executives. They have neither time nor inclination to read this."**

*Observation + operational consequence* compressed into two sentences. No hedging. "Neither time nor inclination" names both failure modes (cognitive load and signal about respect for their time).

> **"If you do not include an executive summary / 'smart brevity' blurb at the top... then you are inadvertently giving a message you don't value their time."**

*The rule* — written as a direct if/then. Assigns the consequence to the sender's action, not the audience's reaction. The word "inadvertently" does real work: it separates intent from impact without excusing the impact.

> **"Put everything you wrote behind a 'The Deep Diver' or 'Go Deeper' (or TL;DR delimiter). Then create an executive summary that explains the big picture and why it matters."**

*The ask* — structural, concrete. Not "be more concise." Tells them exactly what to build and where each piece lives.

**What to notice:** No labeled sections. No "great effort, but..." opener. The phrase "use me as practice" reframes the note as a gift — Joe is the low-stakes test before the real audience. That framing does the relationship work without softening the diagnosis.

### Example B — Giving feedback with evidence, not conclusions (Teams chat, lightly annotated)

> **"Thank you for coming to me directly."**

*One sentence of acknowledgment* — earned and specific. Not effusive.

> **"If you don't mind, just write up all of your concerns, back it up with observations i.e. over-reliance on Product/Program --> Situation Behavior Impact --> conclusion feedback so I am not leading from the same conclusions but see the same evidence."**

*The ask* — precise methodology attached. Joe names the framework (SBI: Situation, Behavior, Impact) not to be academic but because it's the actual artifact he needs to act. The reason is embedded: "so I am not leading from the same conclusions but see the same evidence." That's the operational consequence of underdocumented feedback — it can't be defended or acted on.

> **"'Someone said' usu doesn't carry the argument"**

*The rule* — compressed, almost aphoristic. Parenthetical softener ("Not questioning") does just enough work to signal this is coaching, not rejection.

**What to notice:** Joe is coaching on how to give *him* feedback, not just how to give feedback generally. The ask has a clear output format. The operational consequence is named inside the ask itself — "so I am not leading from the same conclusions." No separate section needed.

## Voice Notes

**What makes coaching notes distinctive in Joe's register:**

- **The anchor is unglamorous.** Joe names the artifact or event flatly — "here is the outline," "you just sent," "this week, and ASAP." No windup.

- **Consequences are named as mechanics, not feelings.** "The predicted completion date is undefendable" not "the team may feel confused." The harm is operational.

- **Rules travel forward.** The best coaching notes leave the reader with a decision rule they can apply next week without Joe in the room. "If X, then Y" is the format. "Be more like Z" is not.

- **The ask is a deliverable, not a disposition.** "Write up your concerns in SBI format." "Put a BLUF at the top." "Set up a bi-weekly ops meeting with [name] by [date]." These are completable. Attitude adjustments are not asks.

- **Confidence is shown, not stated.** Joe doesn't write "I believe in you." He writes "use me as practice" or "putting you in the driver seat" — framing that assigns agency and signals he expects execution, not reassurance.

- **Length matches complexity, not deference.** A two-behavior correction can be three sentences. A structural skill gap gets a full explanation with a framework attached. Neither pads for politeness.
```
