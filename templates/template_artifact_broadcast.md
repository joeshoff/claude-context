register: low-ceremony / informational
audience: team, peer group, or leadership — anyone who needs the artifact, not a discussion
when_to_use: |
  The message's only job is to hand off an artifact. No action is required beyond
  reading or reviewing. Use when dropping a report, dashboard, document, deck, or
  archive into a channel, thread, or inbox. If you need a response or decision,
  use a different template.

## Structural Pattern

```
[ARTIFACT NAME + WHAT IT IS]  →  [LOCATION OR ATTACHMENT POINTER]  →  [ONE-LINE ORIENTATION]  →  [FORWARD ROUTING if applicable]
```

**Rules for this type:**
- The artifact name comes first, or the subject line carries it. Readers know immediately what they're getting.
- One sentence of orientation is the ceiling. Tell them what to look at or what the artifact covers. Do not summarize it.
- If the artifact has internal structure worth naming (e.g., "conclusions are in the Word doc, supporting data in the zip"), name it once, plainly.
- If the artifact will recur or auto-update, say so and say where. That's the only forward-looking content allowed.
- No ask. No discussion invitation. No "let me know what you think."
- Subject line (email): `[Artifact name or series] — [date or version if relevant]`

---

## Template

### Teams Chat / Slack variant

```
[ARTIFACT NAME or brief descriptor]. [LINK or <attachment>]

[One sentence: what's in it / what to look at / how it's organized — only if non-obvious.]

[If recurring: Reports will [drop here / update at cadence] going forward.]
```

### Email variant

**Subject:** `[Artifact name] — [date / sprint / version]`

```
[ARTIFACT NAME] for [SCOPE / PERIOD] is [attached / linked below].

[One sentence of orientation — what the artifact covers, or how it's organized, only if the filename alone doesn't make it obvious.]

[If there's a permanent home: Future [reports / versions] will live in [LOCATION].]
```

## Examples

### Example A — Teams chat, report drop with forward routing

> I have all SEM reports in a folder in our team leadership channel
> "Student Lifecycle Services-50-PE-16-Eng-PLs (Private)".
> My report generation will write to this location automatically so you can see them as they pop out fresh.

**Why it works:**
- Artifact named first (SEM reports), location given immediately.
- One sentence of forward routing — no manual redistribution needed after this.
- Zero ceremony. No "please find attached," no summary of what an SEM report is.

---

### Example B — Teams chat, multi-file archive drop

> Here ya go. Conclusions are in the Word doc and pptx. Supporting docs are archived
> in the attached zip — requirements list, comparison matrix, usability analysis,
> Gartner research analysis.

**Why it works:**
- No artifact title needed because it landed in an active thread with context.
- Internal structure named once so the reader knows where to go for the answer vs. the evidence.
- The list of supporting docs is compressed into a parenthetical — it informs without expanding.

## Voice Notes

**What makes this type distinctive in Joe's register:**

1. **No throat-clearing.** Joe does not write "Attached please find" or "I wanted to share." The artifact or its location is word one or two.

2. **Orientation, not summary.** If the artifact is self-explanatory, Joe says nothing about its contents. If there's a split (conclusions here, data there), he names the split. He does not abstract the findings into the message body — that defeats the pointer-only purpose.

3. **"Ta da" is the ceiling for celebration.** Casual drops get casual delivery. Joe does not editorialize about the artifact's value or the effort to produce it.

4. **Forward routing is a feature, not a footnote.** If the artifact is recurring, the message establishes the permanent location and the cadence. That's load-bearing information, not a PS.

5. **If the audience might have missed it, say so plainly.** "Don't know if y'all saw..." is acceptable context-setting. It is not an apology. It moves on immediately to the pointer.
