  Asking a specific question or requesting a decision/input over Teams chat.

## Structural Pattern

```
[Context sentence — one, only if the question won't land without it.]
[Question. Direct. Specific. If two questions, number them.]
[Constraint or decision rule embedded in the question itself, not after it.]
```

Rules:
- Context is optional. If the question is self-evident from shared history, skip it.
- The question carries the constraint ("given X, can you Y?" not "can you Y? (also note X)")
- If forwarding or quoting someone else's message, the question comes first, quoted material appended.
- No setup paragraph. No softening opener. No close.
- Two questions are allowed. Three are a meeting.

---

## Template

```
[ONE SENTENCE OF CONTEXT — omit if question stands alone.]
[QUESTION 1 — specific, constraint embedded, answerable with a decision or fact.]
[QUESTION 2 — only if needed, numbered, same context scope.]
```

### Variant A — Single question, no context needed

```
[SPECIFIC QUESTION with embedded constraint or scope]?
```

### Variant B — Single question, one sentence of context

```
[CONTEXT: what situation or artifact this is about, one sentence].
[SPECIFIC QUESTION with embedded constraint or scope]?
```

### Variant C — Two questions, shared context

```
[CONTEXT: one sentence.]
Two questions: 1. [SPECIFIC QUESTION]? 2. [SPECIFIC QUESTION]?
```

### Variant D — Question attached to forwarded content

```
[QUESTION — stated before the quoted material.]
[Forwarded/quoted content below or appended.]
```

---

## Fill-in-the-Blank Template

```
[CONTEXT: one sentence describing the situation, artifact, or trigger — delete line if not needed.]
[QUESTION: name exactly what you need to know or decide, embed any constraint that bounds the answer.]
[SECOND QUESTION if needed — number both if so.]
```

**Placeholders defined:**

- `[CONTEXT]` — The situation in one sentence. Include the artifact name, ticket, or event if
  the reader won't have it top of mind. Cut if the question is self-explanatory.
- `[QUESTION]` — The specific ask. Embed scope, constraint, or decision rule inside the
  question itself. "Given you only have X capacity, what would you cut?" not
  "What would you cut?" followed by "keep in mind you only have X capacity."
- `[SECOND QUESTION]` — Only if it shares the same context and both can be answered in
  one reply. Number them. If they require different people or different contexts, send separately.

## Annotated Examples

### Example A — Variant D (question on forwarded content)

> Could you look at this and nominate someone — yourself if you wish — out of the SA group
> to participate? I fear my SEMs are too busy, do not want to lose too much engineering
> muscle, and this needs enough experience to avoid getting trapped in the weeds.

**What's working:**

- Question arrives before the forwarded block. The reader knows what to do before reading the context.
- Constraint is embedded: *out of the SA group* rules out other pools without a separate sentence.
- The reasoning ("SEMs too busy," "can't lose engineering muscle," "needs experience") is
  compressed into one clause, not a bulleted rationale section.
- No close. Appended message does the context work.

---

### Example B — Variant C (two questions, shared context)

> RE: NGLX and WGUid — looks like this is blowing up and I read your statement in the task.
> It sounds impossible to know the size of the ask.
> Would it be possible to know how much reserve capacity impacted teams under you would have
> if priorities do not change? And what lowest-priority items would have to be de-prioritized
> to make it fit?

**What's working:**

- "RE: NGLX and WGUid" acts as the one-sentence context anchor. Situation is established in
  eight words.
- Both questions are decision-enabling, not exploratory. The first question ("how much reserve
  capacity *if priorities do not change*") embeds its constraint. The second is a direct follow-on.
- "Might shorten the decision cycle" is a judgment, not a hedge — Joe is saying why this matters,
  not suggesting it could possibly be useful.
- No close. No "let me know."

## Voice Notes

**What makes teams_inquiry distinctive in Joe's register:**

1. **The question is the message.** Context is scaffolding, not the point. If you find yourself
   writing more context than question, you've written a status update, not an inquiry.

2. **Constraints go inside the question, not after it.** "Out of the SA group" and "if priorities
   do not change" are not caveats — they are scope boundaries that make the question answerable.
   Separate caveat sentences read as hedging.

3. **Forwarded material goes after the ask, not before.** In email convention, context leads.
   In Joe's Teams usage, the ask leads. The reader knows the job before they read the attachment.

4. **Two questions are a hard ceiling.** Example 4 ("Two issues") and Example 3 (two linked
   questions) both stay tightly scoped. When Joe has two questions, he numbers them or sequences
   them with "and." A third question means the issue needs a meeting or a structured document.

5. **No performative openers.** "Happy Friday" does not appear. "Hope this finds you well" does
   not appear. The first word is either a noun, a verb, or a question word.

6. **Frustration is embedded, not staged.** The `!@#$` in Example 2 is not an aside — it is a
   signal that the constraint Joe named earlier was already agreed and is being violated. The
   voice carries the weight without a separate sentence explaining that he's frustrated.
