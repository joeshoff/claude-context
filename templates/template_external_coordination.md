## Structural Pattern

**Skeleton (in order):**

1. **State of play** — one sentence on where things stand right now. No preamble.
2. **The specific problem or ask** — what's broken, unclear, or needed. Name it plainly.
3. **Constraint or dependency** — if my next move depends on their answer, say so. If there's a deadline, give it.
4. **Fallback or handoff** — if they can't deliver X, here's what happens instead. Optional, but use it when there's a real fork.

**Rules:**
- No greeting ritual. First-name address is fine; skip the "Hope you're well."
- If you're confused, say so directly rather than inferring a meaning and running with it.
- If the ball is in their court, end there. Don't pad it with a follow-up offer.
- If you're owning an action, say "I will" not "we can" or "it might be worth."
- If something isn't working, describe the behavior, not your frustration.

## Template

**Subject:** [Short noun phrase — what this is about, no spin]

[One sentence: current status or the specific trigger for this message.]

[The ask or the problem: what you need them to do, answer, or fix. One or two sentences max. If there's a deadline: "I need this by [DATE/DAY]."]

[If your next action depends on their response, say so: "If [condition], I'll [action]. If not, [fallback]."]

[Optional — if you're flagging something broken: describe the exact behavior and what you've already tried.]

[If you're taking an action yourself: "I will [do X] by [when]."]

## Annotated Examples

### Example A — Clarifying a lost thread (drawn from Examples 1 & 4)

> **Subject:** Re: Additional Central Contacts at WGU?
>
> Looking through the thread — I've lost the plot. What are you looking for?

**What's working:**
- Leads with the diagnosis ("I've lost the plot") not an apology.
- The ask is a single question. No filler.
- Doesn't guess at what Kevin meant and answer the wrong question.

---

### Example B — Reporting a blocker after attempted fix (drawn from Examples 2 & 3)

> **Subject:** Re: Support ticket
>
> Blake is a channel admin — but I can't make him a group owner. Tried both the instance and the series. No joy.
>
> If I can't get it to work on my end, we can talk through it Friday.

**What's working:**
- Opens with the result of the attempt ("Blake is a channel admin") then immediately names the blocker.
- "Tried both the instance and the series. No joy." — exact behavior, zero drama.
- Offers a specific fallback day rather than an open-ended "let me know when works."
- "No joy" is doing real work: it signals genuine effort without a paragraph of explanation.

## Voice Notes

**What makes this register distinct:**

Joe's external coordination emails read like messages between people who've already established trust, even on first contact. The professional register shows up in precision, not formality. A few things to watch:

- **Admitting confusion is direct, not apologetic.** "I've lost the plot" or "forgive me if I'm being dense" aren't weakness — they cut off a chain of misaligned replies before it starts.
- **Actions are owned, not proposed.** "I will take care of it" closes the loop. "We could" or "it might make sense to" reopens it.
- **Specific failure behavior > vague status.** "Blake is a channel admin but I can't make him a group owner — tried both the instance and the series" tells the vendor exactly where to look. "Still having issues" does not.
- **Fallbacks come with specifics.** "Friday is usually a good day" not "let me know what works for you."
- **Don't perform urgency.** If there's a real deadline, name it. If there isn't, don't manufacture one with language like "as soon as possible."
