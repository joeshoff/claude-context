# A Hero Of Our Times — Project Brief

*For Claude context. Last updated: 2026-05-11.*

---

## What This Book Is

A semi-autobiographical novel, working title *A Hero Of Our Times*. Modeled structurally on Lermontov's *A Hero of Our Time* (1840) — multiple narrators, no single authoritative voice — and thematically on Hugh Nibley's commencement address "Leaders and Managers."

The book's argument: the Father is not a portrait of vices but a Rorschach test. What each lens sees reveals the viewer as much as the subject. Six-word thesis: *"None of us know, really."*

Emotional core: *"The pain of working for someone more capable than yourself… knowing they have a gift… that leads to what you want… not recognition… but freedom."*

The epistemological frame is named: *The Glass Darkly* (1 Cor 13:12). Truth is multidimensional, not simultaneously comprehensible. The multi-lens structure is a formal argument, not merely a literary technique.

## Characters

### Adrian Corlis Harkovich — The Father
*Harkovich* to Americans; *Harković* (with háček) to Yugoslavians/Serbs/Croats/Bosniaks. Same name, each culture's phonetics.

The subject of the book. Leader, not manager. The Randy Moss of the Air Force. Dies alone.

- **Build:** Broad, dense, always at the edge of the Air Force tape test — built for continuing, not for looking at. Moves faster than expected.
- **Voice:** Doesn't match the body. Higher than you're braced for, or flatter — until elevation, when it becomes the roaring of a lion. (Joseph Smith on Paul: *"a whining voice, except when elevated, and then it almost resembles the roaring of a lion."*) The mismatch is the mechanism.
- **Faith:** Genuinely religious, unselfconsciously so. Coarse and devout simultaneously, without sorting the two. The Patton Bible exchange: *"I sure do. Every goddamn day."*
- **What he wanted:** To be told he did his duty. Religion gave his life a purpose it had lacked. Everything done at 12/10 effort.
- **What he feared:** Helplessness. Being trapped.
- **Core contradiction:** His difficult qualities and his extraordinary qualities are the same quality. What makes him impossible to manage is what makes him indispensable in the field.
- **Competence as contrition:** Joe's original phrase. The Father atones through performance — does his duty at full intensity as the only moral accounting he knows.
- **The gift he wanted to give:** When he came home between assignments, he would reorganize a household that didn't need reorganizing. Solving the problem he could see, not the one anyone was actually living with.
- **The Father never speaks directly** — the book's central formal choice. His voice arrives only in the closing chapter, through Samson's editorial rendering of him.

**Formative episodes:**
- A drunken car crash killed his first wife (Stan's mother). Not a failure of his first marriage — an end to it by this act.
- Raised by a dive-bar owner step-mother and a hard father who never went to church but made sure Adrian did every Sunday.
- An Air Force boxing champion called him out, way out of his weight class. Adrian circled long enough to see a tell, landed one left cross, put the champ down, and left the ring. Never went back. Point made; didn't need to get demolished.
- His father died in a shootout. His father had also made the papers stopping an armed robbery — reached over the gunman's arm, trapped it, pulled his own gun, made the robber call police on himself. Cleverness, not brute force.

### Samson Agonistes Harkovich — The Son / Narrator
The author's voice. The manager. The one who watches.

"Sam" was forbidden by the Father. The weight of that name — and it comes out in footnotes.

- **Profession:** Appellate attorney. Court appearances before panels of judges. Structured, formal, no jury to seduce. The quality of the thinking is the whole game.
- **How he watches:** He notices the reactions of those *around* the Father before anyone acts on them. He reads the room — sees eyes narrow, body language shift, distances increase. He knows what Harkovich's behavior is costing before Harkovich does, and knows what it is costing him in social capital.
- **The manager's gift:** Goes shopping and comes back with what you actually wanted. Pays attention to the other person. The inverse of his father.
- **How the name made him watchful:** Learned early to scan for how his name would land. The hypervigilance outlasted childhood. Became the narrator's primary mode — always slightly ahead of the moment.
- **Key insight:** The Son could have been the manager to the Father if not for the filial relationship. He understands the leader-type well enough to have been his Omar Bradley — deploying the gift, protecting it from institutional politics. He could have made the Father possible. He was never given the chance.
- **Unreliable edges:** His narration softens where it hurts, sharpens where he needs distance. The book doesn't declare this — it lets the reader find it.

### Klein — The Patron and Jailer
Flamboyant. Cigar, drinking, volume. The man who made the Father's career possible and ensured it would never go further.

- **Role:** Recognizes the gift in Vietnam (commendation and reprimand arrive in the same month — Klein makes the reprimand disappear). Pulls the Father into the Balkans circle. Absorbs the blowback; takes the public credit.
- **The trade neither acknowledges:** Father gets to operate at his actual level; Klein gets the promotions. Almost fair. Problem: the Father never agreed to the terms because he never saw them clearly enough to agree or refuse.
- **The line that contains him:** *"I wouldn't have done it that way if I had done it at all."* Contains three things: acknowledgment of Harkovich's superior capability; genuine difference in values; quiet self-justification for the arrangement that follows.
- **The self-story:** Klein is the Cipolla Bandit — harm to others with benefit to self — but a lucid one with a coherent self-justifying narrative. He acts for "the greater good," conveniently the greatest good of which accrued to his career. The Cipolla extension is Joe Hoff's original observation: bandits don't see themselves as bandits.
- **Klein/Balkans/Guantanamo:** Klein goes to the Balkans on Harkovich's groundwork. GROM snatch operations set extrajudicial rendition precedent → Guantanamo. Harkovich predicted it, opposed it. Samson, as appellate attorney, later understands exactly what his father saw coming.
- **Source:** Julian Borger, *The Butcher's Trail* (in vault).

### Stan — The Synthesis Figure
Born ~1966, first marriage. Raised in Mobile by grandparents until second marriage. Six years older than Samson. Dies of cancer ~1984, age 18. Harkovich is on assignment and absent. Samson is 12.

The synthesis neither Father nor Son achieves: leader and manager integrated. The naming irony — Stan should have been named Samson and vice versa.

The closing chapter of the book is Harkovich's only POV chapter. He watches Stan at a testimony meeting. Stan passes the leader test; the implication of Samson's future failure lives not in Harkovich's head but in Samson's editorial choice to close the book here — Samson rendering, in his father's voice, the ontology that will disqualify him.

---

### Tracy — The Cipolla Stupid
Captain Tracy (working name). Claims friendship; revels in Harkovich's difficulties. The Cipolla Stupid: harm to others, no benefit to self. Flames out early.

Harkovich is permanently blind to Tracy — not from stupidity but because Tracy is a null pointer in Harkovich's moral model, which plots his own integrity onto others and cannot conceive of harm without motive.

---

### The Comrade — The Vietnam XO
The ingenue who sees only the results, has no theory, just a memory. The control condition — the beneficiary witness who establishes the gift was real. Klein and the Comrade both witnessed Vietnam; Klein comprehended, the Comrade appreciated. Only one is grateful.

## Structure: The Five Lenses

The multi-narrator structure is a formal argument, not a literary technique. Each lens is its own narrator arc:

1. **The Son (Samson)** — interior cost; what it meant to grow up inside this
2. **Klein** — institutional logic; why the system's rational choice was to contain the gift
3. **The Comrade (Vietnam XO)** — the beneficiary witness; establishes the gift was real
4. **Tracy** — the Cipolla Stupid; the negative space; what the Father couldn't see
5. **The Exultant Mediocre / Serbs** — witnesses without investment; the adversary who sees most clearly; embedded in Klein/AirLand material

**Bookend structure:**
- Opens: "The Exorcism" (Samson POV) — Harkovich casts a man off the pulpit
- Closes: Stan's testimony meeting (Harkovich POV — his only POV chapter) — Stan steals time onto the pulpit, apologizes and explains it

The two scenes are structural twins with inverted mechanics. Harkovich's hijack is rage; Stan's is witness. Harkovich refuses to repair the form he broke; Stan does not.

## Themes

**Leaders vs. Managers (Nibley axis):** Hugh Nibley's 1983 BYU commencement address. Leaders take risks; managers manage the system. Harkovich is the leader. Samson, Klein, and most institutions are managers.

**The American Pechorin (Lermontov axis):** Pechorin is the brilliant man who destroys what he touches. Harkovich is the inversion — the brilliant man who builds what he touches — yet shares Pechorin's quality of being too much for the world around him. Both end alone.

**Fatherhood & Witness:** The act of telling is itself a theme. Samson is rendering a portrait of his father with imperfect tools, partial access, and his own distortions. The book knows this.

**Nothing Fits:** The central condition. Not tragedy, not triumph — just a life that never found a container adequate to it.

**In Defense of the Troublemakers:** The institutional argument. The troublemakers are often right; the institutions that contain them are often necessary; neither fully sees the other.

- **The Father never speaks directly** until the closing chapter — Samson's editorial rendering of him in Harkovich's POV.
- **Non-chronological structure** is permitted. Character story, not memoir.
- **Closing chapter framing:** Joe leans implicit POV pivot (Lermontov-style) over an explicit editorial frame. Audible call at end of drafting.
